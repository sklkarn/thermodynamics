    Section 1.6 Examples

    Example 1.6.1.

    Estimate the total internal energy of \(1.00 \,L\) of nitrogen (\(N_2\)) gas at \(298 \,K\) and \(2 \,atm\) if its temperature is increased by \(10 \,K\) at constant pressure. What is the energy required to heat \(1.00 \,mol\) of nitrogen gas from \(0 K\) to \(298 K\) at constant pressure?
    🔗
    Solution.
    To estimate the total internal energy of 1.00 L of \(N_2\) gas at \(298 \,K\) and \(2 \,atm\) , we can use the ideal gas law to find the number of moles of nitrogen gas. we then calculate the internal energy by
    \begin{equation*} dU = n\frac{5}{2} R(T_2-T_1) \end{equation*}
    From Ideal gas equation, we can find the number of moles of nitrogen gas:
    \begin{equation*} n= \frac{PV}{RT} = \frac{(2 \, \text{atm})(1.00 \, \text{L})}{(0.0821 \, \text{L atm/mol K})(298 \, \text{K})} \approx 0.0816 \, \text{mol} \end{equation*}
    or,
    \begin{equation*} \Delta U = 0.0816 \, \text{mol} \times \frac{5}{2} (8.314 \, \text{J/mol K}) (10.0 \, \text{K}) \approx 17.0 \, \text{J} \end{equation*}
    The energy required to heat 1.00 mol of nitrogen gas from 0 K to 298 K at constant pressure can be calculated using the same formula:
    \begin{equation*} \Delta U = 1.00 \, \text{mol} \times \frac{5}{2} (8.314 \, \text{J/mol K}) (298 \, \text{K} - 0 \, \text{K}) \approx 6200 \, \text{J} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.6.2.

    At \(T = 300 \, K\text{,}\) \(1.00 \, mol \) of \(CO_2\) occupies a volume of \(1.50 \, L\text{.}\) Calculate the pressures given by the ideal gas equation and the van der Waals equation. Given: \(a = 3.59 L^2 \,atm\,mol^{-2} \) and \(b=0.04 /,L mol^{-1} \)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Amount of gas \(n = 1.00 \, mol\text{;}\) Temperature \(T = 300 K\text{,}\) Volume \(V = 1.50 L\text{,}\) Ideal gas constant \(R = 0.08206 L\cdot atm \cdot mol^{-1}\cdot K^{-1}\text{,}\) van der Waals constant \(a = 3.59 L^2\cdot atm\cdot mol^{-2} \text{,}\) and van der Waals constant \(b = 0.0427 L\cdot mol^{-1}\text{.}\) Ideal gas equation:
    \begin{equation*} p_{ideal} = \frac{nRT}{V} = \frac{1\cdot 0.08206 \cdot 300}{1.5} = 16.41 \, atm \end{equation*}
    van der Waal’s equations
    \begin{equation*} \left(p_{vdw}+\frac{an^2}{V^2}\right) \left(V-nb\right) = nRT \end{equation*}
    \begin{align*} or, \, p_{vdw} \amp = \left(\frac{nRT}{V-nb}\right) -\left( \frac{an^2}{V^2}\right) \\ \amp = \left(\frac{1\cdot 0.08206 \cdot 300}{1.5-1\cdot 0.0427}\right)-\left(\frac{3.59 \cdot 1^2}{1.5^2}\right) =15.3 atm. \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.6.3.

    The density of dry air at \(p = 1.0 \, bar\) and \(T = 300 \, K\) is \(1.161 \,kg m^{-3}\text{.}\) Assuming that it consists entirely of \(N_2\) and \(O_2\) and using the ideal gas law, determine the amount of each gas in moles in a volume of \(1 \, m^3\) and their mole fractions.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Pressure \(p = 1.0 \, bar = 1.0 \times 10^5 \, Pa \text{,}\) Temperature \(T = 300 \, K\text{,}\) Density \(\rho = 1.161 \,kg m^{-3}\text{,}\) Total Volume \(V= 1 \, m^3\text{,}\) Molar Mass of \(N_2\) (\(M_{N_2}) = 0.028013 \,kg \cdot\, mol^{-1} \) Molar Mass of \(O_2 \, (M_{O_2}) = 0.031999 \, kg \cdot\, mol^{-1} \) Gas Constant \(R = 8.314 J\cdot mol^{-1}\cdot K^{-1}\)
    🔗
    From ideal gas equation,
    \begin{equation*} n_{total} =\frac{pV}{RT} = \frac{1.0 \times 10^5 \times 1}{8.314 \cdot 300} =40.091 \,mol. \end{equation*}
    Total mass of the sample is simply:
    \begin{equation*} m_{total} = \rho\times V = 1.161 \times 1 = 1.161\, kg. \end{equation*}
    The mole fraction is the ratio of the number of moles of a specific component to the total number of moles:
    \begin{equation} x_a = \frac{n_a}{n_a+n_b} \tag{1.6.1} \end{equation}
    \begin{equation*} m_{total} = M_{N_2}\cdot n_{N_2} + M_{O_2}\cdot n_{O_2} \qquad [\because n=\frac{\text{ mass of substance} }{\text{ Molar Mass}}] \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation} 1.161 = 0.028013 \times n_{N_2} + 0.031999\times n_{O_2} \tag{1.6.2} \end{equation}
    \begin{equation} n_{total} =40.091 = n_{N_2} + n_{O_2} \tag{1.6.3} \end{equation}
    on solving (1.6.2) and (1.6.3), we get \(n_{N_2} = 30.583\, mol \) and \(n_{O_2} = 9.508 \, mol\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \, x_{N_2}= \frac{n_{N_2}}{n_{total}} = \frac{30.583}{40.091} = 0.763 \end{equation*}
    and
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \, x_{O_2}= \frac{n_{O_2}}{n_{total}} = \frac{9.508}{40.091} = 0.237 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.6.4.

    The molecule density of interstellar gas clouds is about \(10^4\) molecules/mL. The temperature is approximately \(10 K\text{.}\) Calculate the pressure. Given: \(k = 1.3806 \times 10^{-23} J \cdot K^{-1} \) (or \(Pa\cdot m^3 \cdot K^{-1}\))
    🔗
    Solution.
    The number density of cloud is
    \begin{equation*} \frac{N}{V} = \frac{10^4 \, molecules}{1\, ml} \frac{10^4 \, molecules}{10^{-6}\, m^3} = 10^{10} \, molecule/m^3 \end{equation*}
    From ideal gas equation:
    \begin{equation*} pV = nRT \, \Rightarrow \, pV = NkT \qquad [\because nR=Nk] \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} or, \, p = \frac{N}{V} kT = 10^{10}\times 1.3806 \times 10^{-23} \times 10 = 1.386\times 10^{-12} \, Pa. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.6.5.

    1. Calculate the amount of gas in moles per cubic meter of atmosphere at \(p = 1 \, atm\) and \(T = 298 \,K\) using the ideal gas equation.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. The atmospheric content of \(CO_2\) is about \(360 \, ppmv\) (parts per million by volume). Assuming a pressure of \(1.00 \, atm\text{,}\) estimate the amount of \(CO_2\) in a \(10.0 \, km\) layer of the atmosphere at the surface of the Earth. The radius of the Earth is \(6370 \,km\text{.}\) (The actual amount of \(CO_2\) in the atmosphere is about \(6.0 \times 10^{16} mol\text{.}\))
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Given: \(R =8.314 Pa·m^3/(mol·K) \)
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. From ideal gas euqtion:
      \begin{equation*} \frac{n}{V} =\frac{p}{RT} = \frac{1.013\times 10^{5}}{8.314 \times 298} = 40.90 mol/m^3. \end{equation*}
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Since volume fraction equals mole fraction for ideal gases, mole fraction of \(CO_2\text{,}\) \(x_{CO_2} = 360 \, ppm \, \text{ (by volume)} = 360 mol/(10^{-6} m^3) = 3.6\times 10^{-4}\) Now, the volume of atmosphere,
      \begin{equation*} V=4\pi \times r^2 \times h = 4\pi \times (6.37\times 10^6)^2 \times 10\times 10^3 = 5.10\times 10^{18} \end{equation*}
      🔗
      Also, total moles of the air in this layer,
      \begin{equation*} n_{total} = \frac{n}{V}\times V = 40.90\times 5.10\times 10^{18} = 2.086 \times 10^{20} \, \text{ moles of air}. \end{equation*}
      🔗
      Moles of \(n_{CO_2} = x_{CO_2} \times n_{total} = 3.6\times 10^{-4}\times 2.086 \times 10^{20} = 7.21\times 10^{16}\text{.}\)
