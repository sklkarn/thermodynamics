    Section 1.2 Heat and Temperature

    Nature obeyed simple and universal laws that can be expressed in mathematical form. The laws of thermodynamics are a set of fundamental principles that govern the behavior of energy and matter in the universe.
    🔗
    A scientific study of the nature of heat began with advent of thermometer. A clear distinction between temperature or degree of hotness, and the quantity of heat is that temperature is an intensive property, while heat is an extensive property. Heat is a form of energy that could be transformed to other forms. Heat is transferred between systems due to a temperature difference. Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles in a substance. Temperature can be measured by noting the change of a physical property of a substance, such as the expansion of a liquid (volume change), pressure of a gas, or the change in electrical resistance of a material.
    🔗
    Heat can be transferred through three main mechanisms: conduction, convection, and radiation. Conduction is the transfer of heat through direct contact between particles, where energy is transferred from the hotter region to the cooler region. Convection is the transfer of heat through the movement of a fluid, where the warmer fluid rises and the cooler fluid sinks, creating a circulation pattern. Radiation is the transfer of heat through electromagnetic waves, where energy is emitted by a hot object and absorbed by a cooler object without the need for a medium. The amount of heat transferred can be calculated using the equation \(Q = m \times c \times \Delta T\text{,}\) where \(Q\) is the heat transferred, \(m\) is the mass of the substance, \(c\) is the specific heat capacity, and \(\Delta T\) is the change in temperature.
    🔗
    Specific heat capacity, \(c\) is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of one unit mass of a substance by one degree Celsius. It is an intensive property that varies with the type of substance and its phase. The specific heat capacity of a substance can be used to calculate the amount of heat required to change the temperature of a given mass of the substance, as well as to determine the amount of heat released or absorbed during a phase change. It is a heat absorbing property of a substance, and it is important in many applications, such as in the design of heating and cooling systems, as well as in the study of thermodynamics and energy transfer. The amount of heat absorbed by the system can be calculated using the equation \(Q = m \times c \times \Delta T\text{,}\) where \(Q\) is the heat absorbed, \(m\) is the mass of the substance, \(c\) is the specific heat capacity, and \(\Delta T\) is the change in temperature. In differential form, the equation can be expressed as
    \begin{equation*} \,dQ = mc\,dT \end{equation*}
    If we take \(mc=C\) then it is called a heat capacity of the substance, and the equation can be written as
    \begin{equation*} \,dQ = C\,dT \end{equation*}
    or,
    \begin{equation} C= \frac{ \,dQ}{\,dT}\tag{1.2.1} \end{equation}
    Heat capacity at constant volume and pressure are denoted as \(C_V\) and \(C_P\) respectively, and heat capacities are generally function of temperature. Heat capacity for a mole of substance is called molar heat capacity, and it is denoted as \(C_m\text{.}\) The \(C_V\) and \(C_P\) are related to each other by the equation \(C_P - C_V = R\text{,}\) where \(R\) is the ideal gas constant.
    🔗
    Latent heat, \(L\) is the amount of heat required to change the phase of a substance without changing its temperature. It is an important concept in thermodynamics, as it explains the energy required for phase transitions such as melting, boiling, and condensation. The latent heat of fusion is the amount of heat required to change a substance from a solid to a liquid, while the latent heat of vaporization is the amount of heat required to change a substance from a liquid to a gas. The latent heat of a substance can be calculated using the equation \(Q = m \times L\text{,}\) where \(Q\) is the heat transferred, \(m\) is the mass of the substance, and \(L\) is the latent heat of the substance.
    🔗
    Heat is different from work as it is a form of energy transfer that occurs due to a temperature difference, while work is a form of energy transfer that occurs due to a force acting over a distance. Both Heat and work are path functions, meaning that the amount of heat transferred depends on the specific path taken during the process. The first law of thermodynamics states that the change in internal energy of a system is equal to the heat added to the system minus the work done by the system \(\,dQ = \,dU + \,dW\text{.}\) This means that heat and work are both forms of energy transfer that can change the internal energy of a system, and they are related to each other through the first law of thermodynamics.
    🔗

    Subsection 1.2.1 Laws of Thermodynamics

    The laws of thermodynamics are fundamental principles that govern the behavior of energy and matter in the universe. The first law of thermodynamics, also known as the law of energy conservation, states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or converted from one form to another. This means that the total energy of an isolated system remains constant over time. The second law of thermodynamics states that the total entropy of an isolated system can never decrease over time, and it can only increase or remain constant. This means that natural processes tend to move towards a state of greater disorder or randomness. The second law gives rise to the concept of absolute temperature, which is independent of the materialproperties. The third law of thermodynamics states that as the temperature of a system approaches absolute zero, the entropy of the system approaches a minimum value. This means that it is impossible to reach absolute zero in a finite number of steps. zeroth law of thermodynamics states that if two systems are in thermal equilibrium with a third system, then they are in thermal equilibrium with each other. This means that if two systems have the same temperature, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other. That is temperatures of all the substances in contact with each other will evantually reach a state of equilibrium. Thermodynamics is developed in terms of absolute temperature.
    🔗
    We can obtain an infinite amount of heat out of the material by performing continuous mechanical work as in Joule’s experiment without changing the condition of water. This means that heat is not a substance, but rather a form of energy transfer. Work is also an energy that is transferred from one system to another in such a way that difference of temperature does not play any role.
    🔗
    External and Internal Work: If the system as a whole exerts a force on its surroundings and a displacement takes place, then work done by the system or on the system is called external work. (e.g. piston pushes out of the cylinder due to gas molecules: which is external work done by the gas on its surroundings). On the other hand, if the system is made up of many particles and the particles exert forces on each other, then work done by the particles on each other is called internal work. (e.g. intermolecular attractions between gas molecules: which is internal work done by the gas on itself). The internal work is responsible for the change in internal energy of the system, while the external work is responsible for the change in the energy of the surroundings. External work is macroscopic concept which involves an interaction between a system and its surroundings. It plays a very inportant role in thermodynamics. Internal work is a microscopic concept which involves an interaction between the particles of the system. Hence, it plays no role in thermodynamics.
